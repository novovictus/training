# CompTIA SecAI+ CY0-001 Initial Diagnostic

## Purpose

This first-sweep diagnostic treats all 60 questions as scored questions and is designed for a 60-minute run. It includes the ten questions from the CompTIA-provided sample practice test and adds fifty original questions using the same short, scenario-driven, four-option format.

Do not use the answer key until the run is complete. Record the selected answer and confidence for every question.

Suggested confidence scale:

- 3 - certain
- 2 - probable
- 1 - uncertain
- 0 - guessed

## Objective targets

The diagnostic is intended to test every major area represented by the CY0-001 objectives without assuming prior mastery.

### Domain 1 - AI concepts and foundations

1. Distinguish artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning, generative AI, and autonomous AI systems.
2. Distinguish supervised, unsupervised, semi-supervised, self-supervised, and reinforcement learning.
3. Identify classification, regression, clustering, dimensionality reduction, anomaly detection, and generation use cases.
4. Distinguish training, validation, testing, inference, fine-tuning, retraining, and continuous learning.
5. Explain features, labels, parameters, hyperparameters, embeddings, tokens, context windows, and vector representations.
6. Distinguish neural-network architectures and common uses, including transformers, convolutional networks, recurrent networks, and generative adversarial networks.
7. Explain foundation models, large language models, multimodal models, and small or domain-specific models.
8. Explain prompt engineering techniques, including zero-shot, one-shot, few-shot, chain-of-thought-style reasoning, role prompting, grounding, and structured output.
9. Explain retrieval-augmented generation, vector databases, embeddings, chunking, retrieval, reranking, and grounding.
10. Explain AI agents, tools, plugins, memory, planning, orchestration, and autonomous actions.
11. Recognize hallucination, bias, overfitting, underfitting, false positives, false negatives, and model or data drift.
12. Interpret common evaluation concepts, including precision, recall, accuracy, F1 score, confusion matrices, robustness, and explainability.
13. Explain MLOps and the AI lifecycle from data acquisition through retirement.
14. Identify the security implications of open-source, proprietary, hosted, on-premises, edge, and cloud AI deployment models.

### Domain 2 - Securing AI systems

15. Identify AI assets, trust boundaries, data flows, attack surfaces, dependencies, and privileged components.
16. Perform AI-focused threat modeling across data, model, application, infrastructure, APIs, users, and third parties.
17. Distinguish prompt injection, indirect prompt injection, jailbreaks, system-prompt extraction, and instruction hierarchy abuse.
18. Distinguish training-data poisoning, feedback poisoning, knowledge-base poisoning, and model poisoning.
19. Distinguish evasion and adversarial-example attacks from poisoning attacks.
20. Identify model extraction, model theft, model inversion, membership inference, and training-data reconstruction risks.
21. Identify sensitive-information disclosure, data leakage, memorization, and cross-tenant exposure.
22. Identify excessive agency, insecure tool use, unsafe actions, and inadequate human approval controls.
23. Identify insecure output handling, generated-code risks, downstream injection, and unsafe automation.
24. Identify denial-of-service, resource-exhaustion, token-abuse, and cost-amplification attacks.
25. Identify AI supply-chain risks involving models, datasets, libraries, containers, plugins, APIs, and vendors.
26. Apply least privilege, separation of duties, identity controls, service accounts, secrets management, and authorization.
27. Apply input validation, output validation, content filtering, sanitization, encoding, and allowlisting.
28. Apply data minimization, masking, anonymization, pseudonymization, tokenization, encryption, and differential privacy.
29. Apply model hardening through adversarial training, robust training, fine-tuning controls, guardrails, and safety tuning.
30. Apply RAG security controls, including source validation, access-aware retrieval, document integrity, provenance, and citation checks.
31. Apply API and application controls, including authentication, authorization, rate limiting, quotas, logging, and abuse detection.
32. Apply secure development and deployment practices to AI pipelines, code, artifacts, containers, and infrastructure.
33. Establish model and dataset provenance, integrity verification, signing, hashing, versioning, and software or AI bills of materials.
34. Protect training, validation, inference, telemetry, and feedback data throughout the data lifecycle.
35. Monitor model quality, security events, drift, anomalous prompts, anomalous outputs, and changes in behavior.
36. Establish secure MLOps processes for approval, testing, deployment, rollback, retraining, and retirement.
37. Conduct red teaming, adversarial testing, penetration testing, abuse-case testing, and safety evaluation.
38. Respond to AI incidents through containment, evidence preservation, rollback, revocation, remediation, and lessons learned.
39. Manage third-party and hosted-model risks, including data retention, vendor access, contractual controls, and shared responsibility.
40. Balance confidentiality, integrity, availability, privacy, safety, resilience, and business objectives in AI control selection.

### Domain 3 - AI-assisted cybersecurity

41. Use AI to support threat detection, anomaly detection, behavioral analysis, and threat hunting.
42. Use AI to enrich, correlate, prioritize, summarize, and triage security alerts.
43. Use AI to support incident investigation, timeline construction, root-cause analysis, and response recommendations.
44. Use AI to support malware analysis, phishing analysis, vulnerability analysis, and code review.
45. Use AI to support vulnerability prioritization using exploitability, exposure, asset value, and threat intelligence.
46. Use natural-language interfaces to query security data while preserving authorization and auditability.
47. Use AI to automate security workflows while maintaining approval gates and human oversight.
48. Evaluate AI-generated detections, queries, scripts, configurations, and remediation instructions before execution.
49. Manage false positives, false negatives, confidence scores, thresholds, and analyst feedback.
50. Protect sensitive information when using public, private, or vendor-hosted AI security tools.
51. Recognize automation bias, overreliance, hallucinated evidence, and fabricated indicators of compromise.
52. Measure effectiveness using operational metrics such as detection quality, time to detect, time to respond, workload reduction, and error rates.
53. Apply AI to identity, fraud, insider-threat, cloud, endpoint, network, and application-security use cases.
54. Preserve chain of custody, evidentiary integrity, reproducibility, and analyst accountability when AI assists investigations.

### Domain 4 - Governance, risk, and compliance

55. Establish AI governance roles, accountability, ownership, oversight, escalation, and separation of duties.
56. Develop acceptable-use, data-use, model-use, procurement, development, deployment, monitoring, and incident-response policies.
57. Perform AI risk assessments, impact assessments, threat assessments, privacy assessments, and business-impact analysis.
58. Categorize and prioritize AI risks using likelihood, impact, exposure, criticality, and risk appetite.
59. Select risk responses, including avoid, mitigate, transfer, accept, and discontinue.
60. Maintain inventories of AI systems, models, datasets, owners, purposes, dependencies, and risk classifications.
61. Maintain documentation such as model cards, system cards, data sheets, decision records, testing evidence, and audit trails.
62. Apply transparency, explainability, fairness, privacy, safety, security, reliability, inclusiveness, and accountability principles.
63. Identify and manage bias, discrimination, accessibility, and disparate-impact risks.
64. Establish human-in-the-loop, human-on-the-loop, and human-in-command oversight appropriate to risk.
65. Apply the NIST AI Risk Management Framework concepts and functions.
66. Distinguish voluntary risk frameworks from binding legal or regulatory requirements.
67. Recognize EU AI Act risk classifications, prohibited practices, provider and deployer responsibilities, and high-risk obligations at an exam-appropriate level.
68. Relate AI governance to privacy, cybersecurity, sector, contractual, records-management, and intellectual-property requirements.
69. Perform vendor due diligence and establish contractual requirements for data use, retention, security, audit, notification, and model changes.
70. Establish continuous monitoring, periodic reassessment, exception management, control testing, and governance reporting.
71. Manage legal, ethical, reputational, safety, and societal consequences of AI use.
72. Plan for decommissioning, data disposition, model retirement, record retention, and residual risk.
